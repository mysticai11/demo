The Problem, Precisely

📉
Clinical scores rely on BMI
When BMI is restricted to normal range, the primary signal collapses. These scores lose their discriminative power entirely.
🔴
Some scores actively invert
NAFLD-LFS ranks the sickest normal-BMI patients as healthier than well ones. A negative correlation is worse than random.
🧱
Standard AI models are unreadable
Black-box ensembles learn arbitrary internal axes with no biological meaning. You cannot ask them what the axes represent.
⚠️
Risk estimates fail the sickest patients

Four distinct technical problems. One population falls through all four cracks at once: normal-BMI adults.

Honesty

Honest Limitations

  • Cross-sectional only. We observe metabolic state at one point in time. We cannot yet track how patients move along the map over months or years.
  • Single survey origin. All data comes from NHANES (US national survey). The model's generalisability to non-US populations, non-English healthcare systems, or different lab assay protocols is not yet tested.
  • Prevalence estimate is exploratory. The 29.89% Dual-Burden figure carries a 95% CI of [0.00M, 64.36M]. The point estimate must not be cited in isolation.
  • Not a diagnostic device. LMSIS is a research screening aid. It has not been validated in a clinical trial and cannot replace physician judgement or laboratory confirmation.

Every number in this work carries its uncertainty range. We say what we don't yet know.
